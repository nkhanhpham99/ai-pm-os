# Pattern analysis

A swept post is raw material. This file turns it into something the user can act on: first seven dimensions per finding, then the cross-cutting pass that names a mechanism.

The discipline throughout: **describe what is observably there, then reason about it separately.** A hook is a quote. A mechanism is your read. Never blur them.

Every candidate has already passed the brief's exclusion gates. A candidate that failed a gate is not analyzed as a mechanism and never counts toward a pattern.

## 1. Hook mechanism (why the scroll stopped)

Capture the hook verbatim first. Then classify. Use as many as genuinely apply; strong hooks often stack two.

| Mechanism | What it does | Tell |
|---|---|---|
| **Recognition** | Names a private experience the viewer thought was theirs alone | Comments like "why is this so accurate" |
| **Hyper-specificity** | One oddly exact detail does what a general statement cannot | Named times, exact objects, precise phrasings |
| **Unresolved tension** | Opens a loop the viewer needs closed | A question or an incomplete situation in the first beat |
| **Taboo admission** | Says what people think but do not post | "I know this is bad but..." |
| **Point-of-view placement** | Puts the viewer inside a scene | "POV:", second person, camera as someone's eyes |
| **Visual anomaly** | Something unexpected holds the eye | Odd framing, unexpected object, a jarring cut |
| **Contrast** | Two states side by side make meaning from the gap | Before and after, then and now |
| **List or number** | Promises a bounded, countable payoff | "Three things", "the fifth one" |

If the hook only works through engagement bait ("comment X and I'll..."), a hashtag wall, or a claim the user's red lines forbid, it goes under **off-brand but notable**. Log it; never adopt it.

## 2. Structure (the beat map)

Write the beats as a short arrow chain in the post's own logic: `hook -> escalation -> turn -> landing`. The beats survive when the subject changes, which is what makes them transferable.

Shapes worth naming:
- **Spiral**: hook, deeper, deeper, no turn. Works when recognition is the whole payload.
- **Turn**: setup, misdirect, reversal. Works when the reversal is earned.
- **Accumulation**: small specifics that add up to one feeling. Save-friendly.
- **Witness**: someone describes a real moment with no imposed structure. Most authentic, hardest to fake.
- **Answer**: a question in the hook, resolved in the landing. Comment-friendly.

Note the landing. A post that ends unresolved invites comments; one that resolves cleanly invites saves.

## 3. Emotional job

One sentence: what feeling does this post do something to? Validate, relieve, name, permit or resolve. A post whose emotional job matches what the user's product does is unusually transferable. A post that merely entertains is craft-only, however well made.

## 4. Why it travels

Pick the dominant one.

| Verdict | Signature | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Save-worthy** | Saves high relative to likes; useful, re-readable, resolves | Reference content that keeps working |
| **Share-worthy** | Shares high; "this is us", sent to one specific person | Every share is a personal recommendation |
| **Comment-bait** | Comments outsized against views; unresolved or divisive | Reach with thin depth |
| **Watch-through** | Long view time, modest engagement | Story and craft strength; hard to repeat at volume |

If the platform does not display saves or shares, say the verdict is **inferred** and name what it was inferred from.

## 5. Relevance tier

Core, adjacent or craft-only, as defined in the brief. Be strict. Inflating core destroys the ranking's only purpose.

## 6. Evidence tier

How much the finding proves, on the same scale market-research uses:

| Tier | What it is |
|---|---|
| **A** | Official statistics, peer-reviewed studies, regulatory filings |
| **B** | Reputable journalism, analyst reports with a stated method, a company's own published data |
| **C** | Social posts, community threads, reviews, ad creative, anecdotes |
| **D** | Undated, unattributed or content-farm material |

**Social posts are Tier C: signal, never proof.** A pattern of Tier C findings tells the user what to try or investigate. It does not establish how many people feel something. A number quoted inside a post is the poster's claim, recorded as a quote.

## 7. Transferability

Could the user run this mechanism with their own material, without copying anyone and without breaking a red line?
- **Yes**: the mechanism is clean and the subject is already theirs.
- **Yes with changes**: name the change ("works, but the original leans on a medical frame; re-cut in everyday language").
- **No**: name the red line it breaks, or say it would require reproducing someone's copy.

Then write the **adaptation**: one or two sentences on how the mechanism applies to the user's own material. If the adaptation could be mistaken for a rewrite of the original, it is not an adaptation.

## The cross-cutting pass: from posts to patterns

A **pattern** has a hard bar: **at least three independent examples**, each passing every gate. Independent means different creators. One creator's three posts is a habit, and a brand plus its own paid creators is one source. With two examples, write "possible, watch it" and say so. Never round two up to a pattern.

For each pattern that clears the bar:
1. **A short, reusable name** for the mechanism.
2. **Evidence**: the examples, linked, with engagement as displayed.
3. **Why it works now**: the mechanism, not the topic.
4. **What the user could do with it**: concretely, this cycle.
5. **Confidence**: High, Medium or Low, with a plain-word reason. Three posts from one niche in one week is Low, however striking.

Then the **negative pass**, where much of the honest value is:
- **What did not travel**: formats that appeared often and underperformed.
- **What changed since the last sweep**: read the previous sweep's header. A sweep that restates standing facts teaches nothing.

## Goal-specific lenses

Weight the analysis by the brief's ranked goals.
- **Content hooks**: dimensions 1 to 4 and 7 carry the weight; seeds come from here.
- **Competitor moves**: Track A and ad-library changes. What they launched, claimed, started or stopped advertising, and any change in positioning, with dates.
- **Feature demand**: restricted to core-tier material. Log only three things, each with a source link: an unmet need stated plainly, a workaround observed (people stitching together other tools to get the job done is revealed behaviour, the strongest demand signal there is), and a complaint about an existing tool. Frame it as **logged demand signal, never a roadmap recommendation**. Do not size it or rank it.
- **Pricing and positioning**: what competitors charge and promise, as published, with the access date next to every price. Prices change; never carry one forward without re-reading it.

## Seeds (when content hooks is a goal)

A seed is a starting point, not a finished post: the mechanism it borrows, the user-specific angle, a suggested format, and the travel verdict it aims for. Keep seeds short. A seed that reads as a finished script removes the judgment that makes content good. If a seed cannot clear the user's red lines, drop it rather than soften it.
